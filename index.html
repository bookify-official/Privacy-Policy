Bookify privacy policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

Short version: Bookify doesn't collect any data about you. There are no Bookify servers, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your books never leave your device. The little Bookify does keep about you (your name, your answers to a few questions, your reading time and, if you sign in, your place, bookmarks, highlights and notes and the list of your books) stays on your devices and in your own iCloud, where we can't see it.

Signing in

Signing in is optional: everything in Bookify works without it. What it adds is sync across your devices (see below). Bookify uses Sign in with Apple. Apple gives the app an anonymous ID for you and, the first time only, the name and email you choose to share (which can be a private relay address). Bookify keeps these in your device's Keychain and in your own iCloud account, so your other devices recognize you. They are never sent to us. Settings → Account → Delete account removes them, and everything synced, from your devices and your iCloud; to stop Sign in with Apple for Bookify entirely, open the Settings app → your name → Sign in with Apple.

Sync across your devices

While you're signed in and iCloud is on, Bookify keeps these in step across your iPhone and iPad:

  • your place in each book, with the book's title and chapter, with a few words of the book from that spot, so your other device can find it even if its copy's text differs a little
  • your reading time
  • the list of your books: each one's title, author and file type, where it came from (for a book from "Find books", its link at Project Gutenberg, OpenStax or the Internet Archive, so another device can download it too), and which of your devices have it, with fingerprints of the file and of a small sample of its text, so your devices can tell it's the same book (they can't be turned back into the text)
  • your bookmarks, highlights and notes, with the short passages they're about

All of it goes only through your own iCloud, using Apple's iCloud key-value storage: it is stored by Apple under your Apple ID, and we can't see it. Nothing is sent to a Bookify server, because there isn't one. Books themselves are never uploaded: each device keeps its own copy. You can turn sync off in Bookify's Settings → Sync; signing out also stops it. What's already in your iCloud stays until you delete your account.

While sync is on, Bookify also uses Apple's Handoff: the book you're listening to, and your place in it, is offered to your other devices nearby that use the same Apple ID, so you can carry on there. This goes directly between your devices through Apple; we can't see it.

The questions when you start

When you first open Bookify it asks a few optional questions (why you read, what you read, how much, and a daily goal). Your answers are used only on your device: to set your daily goal and to start "Find books" on textbooks for students. They are stored on your device and in your own iCloud, never sent to us.

Reading time

Bookify counts how long you listen, by day and by book, to show your goal, streak and reading time. These totals are stored on your device and, while you're signed in, in your own iCloud, so they add up across your devices. We can't see them.

Your books

Books and documents you add are processed and stored only on your device. They are never uploaded to us or to anyone else. Speech is made on your device by the built-in voices; no text is sent to a server.

Photos of pages and scanned PDFs

When you add photos of pages, or a PDF that is a scan, Bookify finds the words with text recognition that runs on your device (Apple's Vision framework). The photos and the text found in them stay on your device. If you take photos with the camera or pick them from your library, Bookify only uses them to read the pages you chose.

Looking up a printed book

In "Find books" you can scan a printed book's barcode with the camera, or type its ISBN, to see whether there's a legal free copy to listen to. The barcode is read on your device; the camera picture isn't saved or sent anywhere. To find the book, Bookify sends only its ISBN (and, to search for a free copy, the title and author it found, or a title you type) to public book catalogs: Open Library, OpenStax, Project Gutenberg and Gutendex, and Google Books when Bookify is set up to use it. These lookups aren't tied to you: there's no account, no identifier and no tracking, and the catalogs see an ordinary request from your device. The answer is kept on your device for 30 days, so the same book can be looked up again without a connection. If you then scan the book's pages, its cover picture comes from Open Library; the pages themselves stay on your device.

Your settings and listening progress

Voice, speed, text size and appearance settings are stored only on your device. Deleting the app deletes them. Your place, bookmarks, highlights and notes are stored on your device and, while you're signed in, in your own iCloud (see "Sync across your devices").

"Find books" searches for the title, author or topic you type, and downloads free books you choose from Project Gutenberg, OpenStax and the Internet Archive, and research papers you choose from arXiv and Europe PMC (which includes PubMed Central). Your search also goes to Open Library, a public catalog, so Bookify can show any book, free or not, with its cover and description; no book's text comes from there. When you look at a result, Bookify asks Open Library, OpenStax, Project Gutenberg and Gutendex about that book, to see if there's a legal free copy, and may ask Project Gutenberg the same about results on your screen. Only the words you search for and the book's title, author or catalog number are sent, and not tied to you. Answers are kept on your device, searches for a day and a book's details for 30 days. A classic's page also asks LibriVox, by its title, whether there's a free recording to link to. Libraries Bookify can only link to (such as OAPEN, the Directory of Open Access Books, HathiTrust and SSRN) open in your browser, with your search if you tap one. "Add from a link" fetches the page or file you enter (including shared Google Docs, Slides, Sheets and Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive files). Those sites see an ordinary request from your device; nothing about you is sent with it. While your device charges, Bookify may make the audio for the next part of your book in the background; this happens on your device.

When you share something to Bookify from another app, it's saved on your device for Bookify to read in. It isn't sent anywhere.

Purchases and tips

Bookify Pro and tips are processed by Apple through the App Store. We don't receive your payment details or your Apple ID. Apple tells the app only whether you own Pro, so it can unlock Pro features. See Apple's privacy policy.

Third parties

Bookify contains no third-party analytics, advertising or tracking code, and it doesn't share data with anyone.

Children

Bookify doesn't collect information from anyone, including children.
